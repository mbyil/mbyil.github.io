Project

Q-Read

Development of a custom PCB-based readout system for colloidal quantum dot (CQD) detectors, integrating signal conditioning, data acquisition, noise filtering, embedded signal processing, and Bluetooth-based (Bluetooth Low Energy / BLE) wireless data transmission to a computer for analysis.

RoleElectronics & Signal Processing & Data Acquisition Engineer
StackKiCAD, ESP32, BLE, Python, Signal Conditioning, LTspice
Year2026
Links

Overview

Every type of system that has a detector must have a read-out system that takes it's values and makes sense of it. Well That even goes for CQD detectors.

I have workes in a MEMS (Micro electronical-mechanical systems) fabrication company which was capable of making advanced CQD detectors and wanted make their own read-out circuit to both analyze their detectors performance and to have their own technology for R&D purposes, to not be bound by constraints.

I was asked to design a chip which would be able to read the values coming in from the CQD detector's 16 data pins one by one repeatedly in a short time frame. Here is what what was so critical about it

The problem

To do so I had to design this chip in a way that it wouldn't got misleading values from the coupling of nearby lines and enviromental EMF. It also had to have almost no leakege on the capasitor or analog components as the signal coming in was very susceptable to disturbance. This meant that I both needed to choose my components very carefully and while designing the PCB layout, make sure no cross coupling EMF would occur.

The approach

To make sure that the data would be correct on the analog side I designen a CTIA (Capacitive Transimpedance Amplifier) that sums the signal coming for a period of T, which would then be divided by T to find the average signal received. On the digital end I decided to use median error correction and Baseline correction which get a measurement before letting the signal and subtracks it from original signal to remove initial charge on the capacitors of CTIA.

When it comes to keeping the signal clean I decided to use ground ring and seperataion of digital/analog compenents from each other to make the EMF as low as possible. Also covered the whole chip in metal except for where the bluetooth antenna would be to remove any enviromental noise. While designing the PCB layou rkept in ming the coupling of the wires so traces, lines and their directions were chosen with utmost care.

Bluetooth part was a bit more tricky. Initially what I though was to use an ESP32 chip with a BLE antenna but after consideration shielding the circuit with a metal cover and using an external antenna with the ESP32 that has an antenna connector instead of it's own was more logical. When the signal is transferred by BLE to a computer I used Python libraries like PyQT and matplotlib to visualize andd analyze the incoming data. İt was filtered here with the median error removal and in the end what we had was a clean signal showing us the value at each receptor's frequency as a comprehensible graph which can be used to make evaluations on the material scanned.

The result

I have designed a PCB circuit that is my own which is capable of both protecting from noise/EMF and amplifying the signal input while filtering out incorrect reads both while having a very high precision and low heating on the PCB traces.

This project has been very helpful to me as I have seen my capabilities in designing something just from an ideal piece of my imagination. Very thankful that I have had the experience and the people that trusted in me being capable of doing it.
